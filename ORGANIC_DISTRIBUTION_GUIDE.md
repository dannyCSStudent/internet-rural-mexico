Organic Distribution Guide — Zero Budget
This guide is designed to help Internet Rural México attract its first meaningful visitors without paying for advertising. It is intended for the current stage of the project: the website is published, Google Search Console is configured, and Vercel Analytics is active.
First: Set Realistic Expectations
No guide can guarantee website visits, clicks, purchases, or referral rewards. Those results depend on each person's actual needs, location, available options, budget, Starlink, and the algorithms used by each platform.

What you can control is publishing useful information, honestly answering real questions, measuring which topics help people, and repeating what works.

The initial goal is not to make the website go viral. It is to help the first people with a real internet problem find a useful answer.
Non-Negotiable Principles
- Help first. Share a guide or answer a question before mentioning a referral.
- Never present yourself as Starlink, a Starlink employee, distributor, or authorized installer.
- Never promise coverage, speed, pricing, installation, rewards, or availability at a specific address.
- Do not automatically post your referral link as a response, and do not send mass, repeated, or unsolicited messages.
- If a conversation leads to a purchase recommendation, use this disclosure:
  "This website is independent of Starlink. Some links are referral links. If you make an eligible purchase through one of them, I may receive a reward from Starlink."

- If someone can get a local, mobile, or fiber service that better meets their needs, say so. Honesty builds trust.

Set Up Your Social Profiles Once
Choose a consistent name, such as Internet Rural México, for Facebook, YouTube, Instagram, or TikTok.

You do not need to open accounts on every platform on the first day. Start with Facebook and one short-video platform you are already comfortable using.

Use this description:
Independent guides to understanding internet options in rural areas of Mexico: mobile signal, fixed wireless, fiber, and satellite internet. We are not Starlink or an internet service provider.

Put the main guide in your profile link:

https://www.internetrural.online/internet-rural

Before publishing, make sure your account name, profile picture, and biography do not use Starlink logos or suggest an official relationship with the company.
Where to Participate for Free
1. Local Facebook Groups
Search for groups by municipality, community, state, or activity.

Examples of searches:
- [municipality] news
- [municipality] community
- [municipality] buy and sell
- [state] ranches
- [state] farmers
- [municipality] internet
Read each group's rules and use its internal search function before posting.

Only participate when the group allows the topic or when someone has already asked about internet service.

One specific, helpful answer is worth more than pasting the same link into ten different groups.
2. Short Videos: YouTube Shorts, Reels, or TikTok
A phone and natural window light are enough.

Record vertically, focusing on one idea per video, lasting 20–45 seconds.

You can publish the same original video on one platform and then adapt it for another. You do not need to create entirely new videos for every social network.

Each video should answer a question on its own. Finish with a simple invitation to visit the guide, not pressure to make a purchase.
3. WhatsApp — Personal Use
Share a useful WhatsApp Status or respond to people who contact you about the subject.

Do not copy and paste messages into contact lists, neighborhood groups, or conversations where nobody requested them.

Ask permission before sending a link through a direct message.
4. Local Comments and Conversations
Look for public posts where people are already asking about:
- Internet signal.
- Video calls.
- Security cameras.
- Online classes.
- Remote work.
Answer the exact question, explain a practical way to check the situation, and share one relevant guide if it adds value.

Never use repetitive comments to pull people away from another platform.
The First 30 Days
The posting schedule should be sustainable.

If you cannot record a video one week, prioritize giving a good answer to a real question rather than publishing something just for the sake of posting.
Week 1: Prepare and Listen
1. Set up one or two social profiles using the description and main link above.
2. Find five to ten relevant local groups. Write down their rules, and do not post in groups that do not permit it.
3. Search for existing questions about internet service, signal, homework, cameras, video calls, or remote work. Answer two questions with practical help, without including a link unless it adds value.
4. Publish your first educational post somewhere the rules allow it.
5. Record a short video on this topic: "Before getting satellite internet at a ranch, check these three things."
Week 2: Publish Reusable Answers
1. Publish a post or answer about testing mobile signal in different locations and at different times of day.
2. Publish a video about checking clear sky visibility, electricity, and total costs before choosing satellite internet.
3. Answer two real questions in permitted communities. Personalize each response.
4. Check Vercel Analytics to see which pages received visits and where those visitors came from.
Week 3: Test Different Needs
1. Publish a short guide for families with students or people who depend on video calls.
2. Publish a video for a farm, ranch, or small business that needs cameras, payment processing, or communication.
3. Publish an honest comparison explaining when it makes sense to start with mobile data or fixed wireless before considering satellite internet.
4. Observe which question generates responses from real people. Write it down as an idea for your next piece of content.
Week 4: Repeat What Works and Review
1. Repeat the format and topic that generated the most useful questions, but use a new example.
2. Publish an answer about weather. Explain that weather can affect wireless and satellite connections, and that performance should never be assumed for a particular home without checking.
3. Review Google Search Console and Vercel Analytics.
4. Choose just one topic for the following month, based on actual questions rather than assumptions.
At the end of the month, a useful result might be knowing which topic interests people, which communities allow meaningful participation, and which guide receives visits.

Do not interpret a purchase or referral reward as a confirmed result of a particular post. The website does not currently measure final Starlink conversions.
Content Ideas That Actually Provide Value
- How to perform a basic mobile signal test before signing a contract.
- Fiber, fixed wireless, mobile data, and satellite internet: what questions should you ask each provider?
- What to check for online classes, video calls, and remote work from a rural community.
- Electricity, backup power, and equipment placement: costs worth considering before subscribing.
- Why clear sky visibility matters for certain satellite internet solutions.
- What to ask a fixed wireless provider about line of sight and installation.
- Security cameras on a farm: internet, electricity, and backup power are three separate problems.
- When a local internet option may be better than satellite.
Do not invent personal experiences, test results, or prices.

If you mention plans, prices, coverage, or referral program rules, verify the information using Starlink's official sources that same day and link to the source. Do not copy temporary prices into the website or social media content.
