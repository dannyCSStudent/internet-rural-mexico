# Internet Rural México — Initial Customer Profile

**Research date:** 2026-09-07  
**Market:** Rural Mexico  
**Initial geographic laboratory:** Michoacán  
**Brand working name:** Internet Rural México  
**Status:** Initial research hypothesis — validate through real conversations and analytics.

## Executive Finding

The strongest initial opportunity is not "everyone in rural Mexico." It is people who live or work in rural/remote properties, have unreliable/slow/expensive/no fixed Internet, have a concrete reason to need dependable connectivity, can justify the cost if it solves an important problem, and are comfortable researching/purchasing online.

The brand should therefore position itself as a **rural connectivity information and solutions resource**, with Starlink as the initial monetization opportunity rather than the entire identity.

## Market Evidence

INEGI's 2025 ENDUTIH results report that **88.9% of people in urban areas and 75.2% in rural areas used the Internet**. The urban-rural difference had narrowed to 14 percentage points over the preceding eight years.

The 2024 ENDUTIH reported **86.9% urban Internet use versus 68.5% rural**, showing both substantial growth and a persistent rural gap.

Rural users are already active online. ENDUTIH 2023 reported rural Internet use for communication at 90.5%, social networks at 89.2%, and information searches at 80.8%.

Starlink itself publishes a Mexican rancher case study describing a rural Sonora ranch using Starlink for production, communication, distribution, and market information.

Current Starlink Mexico service pages position Residential service for homes and describe service intended for remote locations. Current prices and offers must always be checked directly because they can change by location and over time.

## Primary Persona — The Rural Property Owner

**Typical age:** roughly 30–65, used as a marketing hypothesis rather than a qualification rule.

**Location:** rural Mexico; initially Michoacán.

**Property:** rancho, finca, casa de campo, rural home, agricultural property, tourism property, or small rural business.

**Current Internet:** none, cellular data, unreliable fixed connection, slow connection, or an expensive/limited alternative.

**Primary need:** reliable Internet.

**Why:** work, business, security cameras, family communication, education, entertainment, property management.

**Buying trigger:** current Internet prevents something important.

**Biggest objections:** cost, uncertainty, availability, installation, weather/performance.

**Discovery channels:** Google, YouTube, Facebook, WhatsApp, local/community groups.

**What we should sell:** confidence in the decision, not merely a satellite kit.

**What we monetize initially:** eligible Starlink referrals.

## Customer Pain Hierarchy

**Level 1:** Internet is a little slow — low urgency.

**Level 2:** Internet goes out sometimes — moderate urgency.

**Level 3:** Cannot work reliably — high urgency.

**Level 4:** Business depends on the connection — very high urgency.

**Level 5:** No viable alternative exists — extremely strong intent.

Our initial content should concentrate on Levels 3–5.

## Objections To Address With Content

- "Es muy caro."
- "¿De verdad funciona?"
- "¿Y si llueve?"
- "¿Cuánto consume de luz?"
- "¿Lo puedo instalar yo?"
- "¿Está disponible donde vivo?"
- "No sé si vale la pena."

Never guarantee availability, speed, uptime, or pricing. Direct users to current Starlink information when a decision depends on current location or plan details.

## Initial Geographic Strategy

### Stage 1 — Michoacán

Use Michoacán as the real-world laboratory. Possible research/content themes include rural Internet, Starlink for ranchos/fincas/casas de campo, rural business connectivity, and local use cases around agricultural and tourism areas.

Potential local topics include Zamora, Jacona, Morelia, Tiquicheo, and other locations validated by actual demand. Do not create thin SEO pages just by changing a city name.

### Stage 2 — Western/Central Mexico

Expand only after evidence supports it. Candidate states include Jalisco, Guanajuato, Guerrero, Colima, Nayarit, Querétaro, and Oaxaca.

### Stage 3 — National

Expand nationally after the local acquisition model is repeatable.

## Positioning

Do not position the brand as a "Starlink referral site."

Working positioning:

> **Internet Rural México ayuda a personas, familias y negocios en zonas rurales a encontrar, entender y elegir soluciones de conectividad para sus propiedades.**

Short version:

> **Internet para donde las opciones tradicionales no llegan.**

These are working hypotheses and should be tested before final branding.

## Customer Discovery Plan

Before building advanced software, interview 10 real people:

- 3 rural homeowners
- 2 ranch/finca owners
- 2 rural business owners
- 2 remote workers
- 1 person building a rural property

Ask:

1. ¿Qué Internet tienes actualmente?
2. ¿Qué es lo que más te molesta de tu Internet actual?
3. ¿Cuántas personas usan el Internet?
4. ¿Para qué lo usas principalmente?
5. ¿Cuánto pagas actualmente?
6. ¿Qué pasa cuando el Internet falla?
7. Si tuvieras Internet rápido y estable, ¿qué harías que ahora no puedes hacer?
8. ¿Has escuchado de Starlink?
9. ¿Qué te detiene de contratarlo?
10. ¿Qué información necesitarías antes de decidir?

Do not sell during the interview. Capture exact language and update this profile with evidence.
